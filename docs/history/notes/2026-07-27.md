## Is O(log N) even possible?

Yesterday ended with query complexity as the one thing going against me: O(sqrt(N)) vs log N for
HNSW. O(log N) was always the goal, and a tree over the centroids should give it, but first I
wanted to know whether the data allows it at all.

### Oracle

For a fixed cell size, across 124k -> 999k (8x data), same 1,000 queries, exact top-10:

* **floor**: how many cells the true top-10 actually sit in, i.e. what a perfect router would
  scan
* **achieved**: how deep centroid-cosine ranking has to probe

The floor doesn't grow with N:

| N | cells (c=128) | cells holding the top-10 | vectors in them |
|---|---|---|---|
| 125,000 | 976 | 4.39 | 688 |
| 250,000 | 1,953 | 4.24 | 701 |
| 500,000 | 3,906 | 4.19 | 714 |
| 999,000 | 7,804 | 4.01 | 698 |

8x the data and the same work; it even drifts down slightly as density rises. The necessary scan
is O(1) in N, so the geometry doesn't block O(log N).

### Cell size sets the exponent

Fit `scanned ~ N^alpha` over the same range:

| cell size | alpha at R@10=0.95 | alpha at R@10=0.99 |
|---|---|---|
| 128 | 0.08 | 0.30 |
| 256 | 0.17 | 0.46 |
| 512 | 0.19 | 0.38 |
| 1024 | 0.23 | 0.46 |

0 is flat, 0.5 is sqrt(N), 1 is linear. The old `recommend()` targeted sqrt(N) cells, so sqrt(N)
vectors per cell, which hard-codes O(sqrt(N)). That was one line.

### Headroom

At c=128, N=999k: floor 698 vectors, achieved 52,739. 75x. That's the main target now.

|---|---|---|
| HNSW over centroids beats a flat scan | 2x worse: 3,677 qps flat vs 1,874 graph at R@10 >= 0.95, and lower recall | routing wants the top 1-7% of cells; greedy search visits much of the graph anyway, by pointer chasing, while the flat version is a 500 KB sequential scan. `MIN_CELLS_FOR_GRAPH` raised from 4,096 to 65,536; the real crossover isn't measured |
| the tree wins at 1M | no, level within noise (+/-4%) | at 7,804 cells flat routing is 488 KB against a 1 MB scan, so at most ~1.2x to gain. The tree's gain is sqrt(M)/b: 2.7x at 1M, 8.8x at 10M, 27x at 100M. It's for 10M+ |
| a beam of 8 chapters is enough | no; cells are spread over 13.4 of 88 chapters, 32 needed for 95% | the summary is lossy by ~2.4x at every level |
| beam proportional to need/fanout | cost 1.7 recall points | wanted cells don't nest into need/fanout parents; it's ~4.8, not 89. Beam is now a fraction of need (`DEFAULT_BEAM_FRAC`) |
| int8 scan + SQ8 rerank as the memory option | dominated: 400 B/vector, 0.9934, 69 qps vs 464 B, 0.9937, 205 qps for binary + SQ8 | per-dim SQ8 needs an f32 conversion per element |

## Concurrency

`FlatCellIndex` mutations take `&mut self`, so pyo3 raises "Already borrowed" if a write comes in
while a search is running. Worse, the insert just didn't happen and `len()` was wrong.

`ConcurrentIndex` (`src/concurrent.rs`) is immutable segments plus one mutable head, with snapshot
reads (the Lucene/Milvus layout). It works because cells don't reference each other; you can't
split a graph like that without cutting edges.

| | write during reads | `len()` | reader qps before / during / after |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FlatCellIndex` | raises | 94,000 (wrong) | 38,868 / 0 / 33,523 |
| `ConcurrentIndex` | fine | 98,700 | 38,870 / 37 / 33,585 |

Same recall as `FlatCellIndex` (0.9374, rows identical).

The head is on every read path, so a write still stalls reads while it runs (110 ms for 5,000
inserts). Fixing that means staging writes and publishing on refresh (like Lucene NRT) or a
chunked append-only arena. Leaving it for now; it's not what the benchmarks measure and faiss
doesn't have it either.

## Next: a better summary than the centroid

This is the only thing that goes after the throughput gap.

The true top-10 sit in 4.01 of 7,804 cells, and centroid ranking needs 60 probes for R@10 0.95 and
404 for 0.99. 75x. The same ~2.4x loss happens at every tree level.

A mean is the wrong thing to route on. Routing wants "could anything in here match", an upper
bound; a mean answers "what is this on average".

It's cheap to try: summaries live on the centroid side, 1/128 of the data. A summary 4x richer is
~2 MB next to 553 MB, and recall ceiling, MRR, memory, build, delete, churn and filters are all
unaffected.

Bytes (999k, np=512, rf=8): scan 4.2 MB (64%), routing 2.1 MB, rerank 307 KB. 10x fewer probes
would be ~1.4 MB, 4.7x fewer bytes, enough to cover the 3-5x.

Candidates (the first two failed before at 315-1000 vectors per cell, which may not hold at 128):

1. **mean + angular radius**: an upper bound on the best member. `r_bound`/`r_max` are already
   computed in every cell and unused. Failed before because one outlier inflated the radius;
   cells are much tighter at 128.
2. **2-4 sub-centroids**: +2.4 points at np=8 for 1.2-1.3x bytes before. That might flip now that
   routing is the bottleneck.
3. **element-wise max over members**: cheap, bound-like, and I haven't seen it tried for IVF.

`oracle_nprobe.py` (floor vs achieved) and `branch_concentration.py` (per level) are ready, ~40 s
a run at 1M. Target: 60 probes down toward 4.
